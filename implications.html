Patterns Populations Management — PopMan

Patterns Populations Management (PopMan), nicknamed Pops, manages encountered combinations as explicitly identifiable, attributed, and reusable computational objects. Its pattern store natively accepts, combines, prunes, and infers over encoded multimodal inputs.

Pattern populations grow, forget, reorganize, associate, and activate across levels of complexity. Distributed population evidence and explicit symbolic identities support the same intelligence contract across sensory and effector channels:

Input matrixSensory channels
→
Brain / processorPopMan pattern populations
→
Output matrixEffector channels

The contract describes how the architecture connects perception, processing, and action. The experimental applications below remain unfinished.

Two Questions Follow: 3.1. Which problems of conventional AI might this architecture address? (14 opportunities) ↓ 3.2. Which biological and psychological phenomena might it help explain? (26 candidate targets) ↓

It also saves and restores whole learned World Models. These operations illustrate parts of the opportunities below; automatic expert extraction and distributed learning remain architectural proposals.

Section 3.1 // Conventional AI Bottlenecks

Which problems of conventional AI might this architecture address?

14 Architectural Opportunities

Multimodal learning is native to the pattern store

PopMan uses the same pattern-management operations to acquire, combine, prune, and infer over multiple encoded input channels. The channels can describe different measurements or different kinds of sensory evidence.

  • MNIST: pixel patterns plus gaze movement combine visual observations with the displacement of the sensor.
  • Stox: open price and volume, alongside high, low, and close, supply distinct market-input channels.

These experiments are not completed. Their unfinished status is separate from the store's native ability to handle multimodal patterns. Clue 7: multimodal inputs →

Continual learning without catastrophic forgetting

The stability–plasticity dilemma

Use the same store operations across input modalities: acquire and combine patterns, reinforce useful evidence, weaken or prune selected structures, and infer from retained populations. Retention and adaptation can be managed together as experience accumulates.

Limited bidirectional associative access

Use one substrate for recognition, recall, prediction, and generation: infer a whole from its parts, a missing part from context, or a likely continuation from preceding evidence.

Opaque representations and inference

Preserve the identities, associations, and contributions of participating patterns, so that even when thousands of detectors contribute to a decision, their contributions can be inspected. Expensive monolithic model storage and activation

Difficult distributed learning and model merging

Combine independently acquired populations through compatible symbolic keys and mergeable attributes. With well-defined update semantics, distributed discovery need not exchange full training datasets or average global weight matrices.

Context-dependent expertise and transfer

Scaling beyond available working memory

Use RAM for active learning and inference, persistent storage for accumulated experience, and hierarchical reuse so that complex patterns need not each be stored as independent objects.

Rigid handling of heterogeneous inputs

Consume, combine, prune, and infer over encoded multimodal inputs within the native pattern store. What Is and Is Not Being Claimed

These are architectural opportunities, not declarations of universal superiority. Several have been explored in limited experiments; others remain engineering conjectures. Modern AI already has partial or alternative solutions to portions of these problems (replay and regularization for forgetting, expert routing and retrieval for conditional computation, model merging for combining models).

The proposed advantage is their possible integration within one continually changing computational substrate.

Neuronal coincidence detection and integration

How thousands of graded, spatially distributed, and temporally overlapping synaptic contributions can produce selective output events. A spike identifies an integration outcome, not the exact combination responsible.

Independent evidence: Azouz & Gray (2000): coincidence sensitivity. The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

3.2 #2
Tier A

Dynamic receptive fields and selectivity

How a neuron or population can respond to several related input configurations, and how its effective receptive field changes with synaptic strength, activity, context, and structural adaptation.

Independent evidence: Brecht et al. (2003): dynamic receptive fields. The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

3.2 #3

Sparse and distributed population coding

How many overlapping detectors can jointly represent an object, category, or event without requiring one neuron per possible combination.

Independent evidence: Sparse and distributed coding of episodic memory (2014). The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

3.2 #4

Associative memory and content-addressable recall

How partial cues reactivate previously established populations, so that a remembered object or experience can be reconstructed from incomplete evidence.

Independent evidence: Nakazawa et al. (2002): partial-cue recall. The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

Pattern separation and pattern completion

How similar experiences can yield distinguishable representations while incomplete observations still recover a familiar whole.

Independent evidence: Leutgeb et al. (2007): pattern separation; Nakazawa et al. (2002): pattern completion. The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

3.2 #7
Tier A Clue 2 →

Working memory and persistence

How circulating or sustained activity can preserve information after the sensory input disappears, making it available to subsequent computation.

Independent evidence: Funahashi et al. (1989): delay-period activity. The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

3.2 #10
Tier A Clue 5 →

Synaptic learning and structural plasticity

How local changes in connection strength, connectivity, and activation thresholds can progressively shape a network's useful pattern populations.

Independent evidence: Trachtenberg et al. (2002): structural plasticity. The proposed PopMan account remains to be tested.

What This Does Not Explain

These are candidate explanatory targets, not established biological explanations. Tier A phenomena (such as integration, plasticity, associative recall, and temporal persistence) have substantial independent biological support; Tier C accounts (such as intuition, dreaming, curiosity, symbolic operations, hypnosis, humor, awe, and wanting vs. liking) involve additional conjectures.

The architecture does not yet explain subjective experience, consciousness, emotional valence, or the full biological mechanisms of motivation. Items 22–26 ask what computational signals might accompany these states, not what they feel like. Nevertheless, it offers a computational framework in which these questions can be formulated and investigated.

Synthesis // Closing Thought

The Curse Is Also Part of the Solution

The goal is neither to manufacture an exact biological neuron in silicon nor to declare an entirely new theory of intelligence. It is to identify a collection of computational principles compatible with what we know about biological neural systems, and to test whether their combinations reproduce increasingly interesting capabilities.

We are particularly interested in the relationship between three things:

PILLAR 01 Biological Plausibility
PILLAR 02 Computational Feasibility
PILLAR 03 Observable Intelligent Behavior

Agreement with biology does not establish correctness. Computational success does not establish biological equivalence. But if the same relatively simple combinatorial mechanisms can account for an expanding collection of computational behaviors while remaining compatible with biological observations, they deserve further investigation.

We started with the curse of combinatorics. Perhaps the curse is also part of the solution. Fight combinatorics with combinatorics.
